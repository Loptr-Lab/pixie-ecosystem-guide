01 / FIND THE REAL BLOCKAGE

What is keeping you from creating?

Name the task, not the whole career.

“I need to get organised” is difficult to act on. “I need a producer to review one track before I book a session” gives you something to do. Start where the work has stopped.

Choose one need

Find: I need a relevant resource, collaborator or professional.
Prepare: I need to explain the project, gather records or ask better questions.
Follow through: I know the next action, but I keep losing the context.
Learn: I need a small task that develops a specific skill.

A ten-minute reset

Write the result you need in one sentence. Identify the decision that blocks it. Find the file, link or message that explains that decision. Decide who can move it forward. Choose one action you can finish in your next available work session.

Try it

“I am making ______. Before I can ______, I need ______. The next person needs to see ______. My next action is ______.”

Keep the scale human

A draft can be enough for a first conversation. Set a time limit that fits your energy and access needs. A useful next step should reduce the burden of returning to the work.

03 / PREPARE BEFORE YOU CONNECT

Make the request easy to understand.

Your collaborator or professional brief

Keep this in your own notes. Copy only the parts needed for the conversation. A public introduction can be short; sensitive records belong in an agreed private channel.

The six details that help

Work: What are you making, and for whom?
Need: What specific result or decision do you need help with?
Scope: What is included, and what is outside this request?
Timing: Is there a real deadline or a flexible target?
Budget: What can you pay, or are you asking for a quote?
Access: What format, device or communication method helps you participate?

Before choosing someone

Check their relevant experience and the source of any credentials. Ask about availability, fees, conflicts and what they would need to assess the work. For regulated services, use the relevant official professional register. A profile badge alone does not answer these questions.

An opening you can adapt

“I am developing [project]. I need help with [bounded task] by [date or flexible target]. I found your work through [source]. Is this within your scope, and what would you need to estimate the cost? I can share a short brief privately.”

Define the arrangement

Before work begins, agree on deliverables, cost, review points, credit, permissions and how either person can stop. Use the appropriate professional for legal, tax or other regulated decisions.

04 / KEEP THE RECORD

Help your future self return to the work.

Keep one small checkpoint

Record the project, today’s decision, the source link, what changed, the blocker and the next action. Add a date. Store the checkpoint beside the work or in its established coordination record.

A portable note

Project: ______
Decision and date: ______
Evidence or file: ______
What I finished: ______
What is blocked: ______
Next action / owner: ______
Return or review date: ______

Public progress, private substance

A public issue can describe a broken link or a contribution. It should not contain private correspondence, contracts, health information, passwords or somebody else’s contact details. Keep a separate private brief when the task needs one.

What PIXIE can help with today

For creative help, the Companion can prepare an editable enquiry and a follow-up file you choose to export. Importing a follow-up requires fresh review. Nothing is sent automatically. For contributions, it can prepare a public GitHub checkpoint draft. You review it and submit it using your account. Keep the issue URL so the work can be resumed from that shared record. A prepared draft is not a saved issue, an assigned task or maintainer acceptance.

Keep authorship attached

For a useful asset record, note who made it, where it came from, what permission covers your intended use and what remains unresolved. Permission to inspect or download an asset is not automatically permission to publish it.

05 / WHAT WE ARE BUILDING TOWARD

A studio that helps people keep creating.

The direction

Loptr Lab is testing the viability of an open-source multimedia training studio. Existing games, stories, research and creator tools are the testing ground. PIXIE is the companion intended to help people join, learn, coordinate and return with context intact.

The next application workflow

Discover relevant help. Prepare an enquiry. Keep the source and permissions attached. Choose what to share. Record the next action. Resume without rebuilding the whole conversation. This is the development direction, not a promise that every step is automated today.

What matters to us

People and creative practice come first. Accessibility belongs in the working process. Funding should sustain the people and maintenance behind it. Sponsorship does not buy suitability rankings, ownership or authority over participants. Our long-term vision includes universal basic income.

Where partners can help

A useful pilot has a defined audience, one deliverable and a review point. Possible conversations include a creator enquiry workflow, a workshop companion or a small accessibility review. Scope, budget, responsibilities and permissions must be agreed before delivery.

What still needs to be proven

Can people find suitable work? Does training lead to useful contributions? Can collaborators resume with less repeated explanation? Can paid work sustain the studio? We are building evidence for those questions, including what fails.

06 / CHOOSE YOUR NEXT STEP

Bring one project. Name one need.

For creators

Start with Made-Sick.org for resource discovery. Use the brief on page 4 to make the next conversation clearer. You do not need to share the whole project archive to make a first enquiry.

For contributors

Begin at Loptr Lab Training and the ecosystem companion. Choose one bounded task. Current open review and contribution opportunities are voluntary and unpaid; any paid work needs a separate signed agreement.

For event teams and potential partners

Have educational material that people cannot easily use after an event? A possible first pilot is one approved session turned into a practical companion: edited summary, source links and an action worksheet. Availability, speaker approval, accessibility checks, cost and publication rights would be agreed together.

Start a specific conversation

questions@loptrlab.com
Subject: Creative workflow enquiry
Tell us: what you are making; the task that stalls; whether you are seeking a resource, contribution or scoped paid pilot; your timing; and your preferred reply format. Keep the first message brief and leave sensitive documents out.

Your choice stays yours

An enquiry is a request for a conversation. It does not subscribe you to marketing, enrol you in a project or guarantee a service. You can use the public resources without making contact.
